Product vs. experiment

The product

Accounts, camera calibration, live tracking, Focus Score, history, analytics and the Schedule department. These features are software-tested (automated tests check they behave as specified).

The experiment

Measures one engineering question about the attention engine only: how accurately does it detect distraction, and does personal calibration improve that accuracy across different camera placements and sensor-noise levels?

A passing software test shows the code does what it was designed to do. It is not evidence that the system measures real students' attention reliably. The current experiment uses simulated "virtual students"; no human participants were involved.

Engineering question & variables

  • Independent variable 1: calibration mode (personal calibration vs. population-default thresholds).
  • Independent variable 2: sensor-noise level (low / medium / high landmark jitter and detection dropouts).
  • Dependent variables: frame-level F1, precision, recall, specificity and accuracy for "distracted"; 5×5 confusion matrix; episode detection rate; detection latency (s); false alarms per on-task hour; Focus Score absolute error (percentage points).
  • Controls: identical virtual students, behaviour timelines and random seeds across conditions (paired design); 10 frames/s; 20-minute sessions; fixed engine version and parameters.
  • Pre-specified analysis: Wilcoxon signed-rank test on paired per-student F1 at each noise level, Holm-corrected, with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals and effect sizes.

The algorithm

  1. Features (in the browser). MediaPipe Face Landmarker gives 478 face landmarks and a head-pose matrix. From these: head yaw and pitch (degrees), eye aspect ratio (EAR), and horizontal/vertical iris offset.
  2. Calibration. Centre look (10 s) sets your neutral pose and open-eye EAR; four corner looks (3 s each) set how far you turn to see your screen; a desk look (4 s) checks the direction of "down".
  3. Frame rules (first match wins): no face → Not detected; EAR < 0.65 × your open-eye EAR → Eyes closed; pitch below your neutral by more than the down threshold → Looking down; |yaw deviation + 30° × iris offset| above the away threshold → Looking away; otherwise Focused.
  4. Temporal filter. A non-focused state is reported only after it lasts its onset time; return to focused needs 0.5 s.
Focus Score = 100 × (time reported FOCUSED) ÷ (session time)

Known limitations

  • The virtual-student model is a set of assumptions (behaviour durations, head/eye movement, noise). Results describe performance on that model, not on real people.
  • Head and eye direction show where someone is looking, not whether they are thinking about the work. Looking down to write notes, or reading a phone held near the screen, can be misclassified.
  • The iris-offset scale (±1 ≈ ±30°) and pitch sign are not yet validated against measured ground truth on real cameras.
  • Lighting, glasses, camera quality and skin tone can affect landmark detection; these have not been tested.
  • Any future testing with people requires approval under school and ISEF human-participant rules (IRB/SRC) before data collection.
